Slack connects two ways, and the way you pick decides what the connection can do.

## Connecting

- **Incoming webhook** — paste a webhook URL in **Accounts → Connect → Slack**. Simplest, write-only: it can post messages and nothing else.
- **OAuth** — install the FoPost app into the workspace and pick a channel. This unlocks file uploads, thread replies, deletion, and reading reactions back.

Either way you need permission to add integrations in the workspace.

## What you can publish

| Limit | Value |
|:------|:------|
| Message length | 40,000 characters — Slack silently truncates beyond its own display limits rather than erroring |
| Files | Up to 10 — images, MP4, or PDF, 20 MB each (OAuth connection only) |

Messages render in Slack formatting: a bold title, the body, and a **Read more** link when the post has one. On an OAuth connection, thread blocks publish as replies in the message's thread; a webhook cannot thread.

## Analytics

On an OAuth connection, FoPost reads back reactions and reply counts per message. A webhook connection reports nothing — it is write-only.

## Deleting

Deleting the post in FoPost deletes the message — OAuth connection only. A webhook cannot delete what it posted.
