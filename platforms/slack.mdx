Slack connects two ways, and the way you pick decides what the connection can do.

## Connecting

- **Slack app** (recommended): in **Connections → Slack**, choose **Connect Slack App**, approve the install, then pick the channel in FoPost. Public channels are joined for you; for a private channel, invite the app to it first so it shows up in the list.
- **Incoming webhook**: paste a webhook URL in the same dialog. Simplest, write-only: it can post messages and nothing else.

Either way you need permission to add apps in the Slack workspace. To post to a different channel later, open the account and choose **Manage Account**.

## Posting identity

An app connection can post under its own name and icon instead of the app's. Open the account in **Connections** and set **Name** plus either an **Icon URL** or an **Icon Emoji** such as `:rocket:`. Every post and inbox reply from that account uses them. Over the API, it's `PATCH /v1/accounts/{id}/slack/identity`; see [Accounts](/api/accounts).

## What you can publish

| Limit | Value |
|:------|:------|
| Message length | 40,000 characters; Slack silently truncates beyond its own display limits rather than erroring |
| Files | Up to 10: images, MP4, or PDF, 20 MB each (app connection only) |

Messages render in Slack formatting: a bold title, the body, and a **Read more** link when the post has one. On an app connection, thread blocks publish as replies in the message's thread; a webhook cannot thread.

## Inbox

An app connection feeds the [inbox](/guide/inbox) as things happen:

- **Direct messages** to the app, which you can answer from FoPost
- **Replies in the thread** of a message FoPost posted, shown as comments
- **Mentions** of the app in the connected channel

You can also start a DM with anyone in the Slack workspace: `GET /v1/accounts/{id}/slack/members` lists them, and a member's `id` is the handle to message. Other chatter in the channel stays out of the inbox. A webhook connection has no inbox.

## Analytics

On an app connection, FoPost reads back reactions and reply counts per message. A webhook connection reports nothing; it is write-only.

## Deleting

Deleting the post in FoPost deletes the message, on an app connection only. A webhook cannot delete what it posted.
