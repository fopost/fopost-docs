Mastodon is federated, so connecting starts with naming your instance. FoPost registers itself with that server and sends you to its own authorisation screen — any instance works.

## Connecting

**Accounts → Connect → Mastodon**, enter your instance's address, approve on your server. Tokens do not expire on stock Mastodon, so a healthy connection stays healthy.

## What you can publish

| Limit | Value |
|:------|:------|
| Post length | 500 characters |
| Media per post | Up to 4 items — images, video, or audio |
| Image size | 16 MB (JPEG, PNG, GIF, or WebP) |
| Video | MP4 or WebM, up to 99 MB |
| Audio | MP3 |
| Alt text | Up to 1,500 characters per item |
| Links | Count as 23 characters regardless of length |

These are stock Mastodon limits, and they are what FoPost enforces — an instance configured to allow more still gets posts within the stock bounds. Hashtags and a link are appended after the text. Thread chains are supported: blocks written as a thread publish as replies to each other.

Per post you can also set **visibility** (public by default), mark media **sensitive**, and add a **content warning** — the same controls Mastodon itself offers.

Video and audio go through the instance's processing; FoPost waits for it to finish, and a file the server is still chewing on after two minutes fails with a message to try a shorter or smaller file.

## Analytics

Per post: favourites, boosts, and replies. Per account: followers, following, and post count. Posts made natively on your instance are included in [analytics](/guide/using-fopost/analytics). Competitor tracking works — track any public account your instance can see.

## Inbox

Mentions and replies land in the [inbox](/guide/using-fopost/inbox), and you can reply from there — the reply is public and addressed to the author.

## Deleting

Deleting the post in FoPost deletes it on your instance.
