The calendar is where the plan becomes visible. Month or week, every scheduled and published post in place, colour-coded.

## Drag to reschedule

Move a post to a different day or slot by dragging it. Nothing else changes: the content, the accounts, and the per-network overrides come with it.

## Colour by label

[Labels](./labels) colour the calendar, so a campaign, a client, or a content pillar is a colour rather than something you read post by post. Filter to one and the rest fades out.

## The gaps are the point

A calendar that renders the whole month makes a four-day silence obvious in a way a list never does. That, and noticing you have queued five posts on Thursday and nothing on Tuesday, is most of what the view is for.

## Filters

Filter by account, label, or status. Useful combinations:

- One client's label, to see only their month
- Status **failed**, to find what needs attention
- One account, to check you are not overposting on the network that punishes it

## Status at a glance

Each slot shows where the post is: draft, scheduled, publishing, published, partially failed, or failed. Failures stand out rather than sitting quietly in a list.

## Create in place

Click an empty slot to compose with that date and time already filled in. Faster than opening the composer and setting a time.

## Unscheduled

Drafts with no date sit in a tray beside the calendar. Drag one into a slot to schedule it, which is the natural way to work through a backlog of half-finished ideas.

## Several workspaces

The calendar is per workspace. An agency switches workspace to see one client at a time, which is the same isolation that stops a post ever landing on the wrong account.
