# Owl Agent

The agent is the AI surface. One chat, at **Agent** in the sidebar, that can reach the whole workspace: your accounts, your posts, your media, your [sources](./sources), your [brands](./brand-voices), your analytics.

## What you can ask for

Real requests, not prompts:

- "Draft five posts about the pricing change, native per network, spread over next week"
- "Turn the launch article into a LinkedIn post and an X thread"
- "What did we publish last month that actually got engagement?"
- "Schedule the approved drafts for the mornings"

It starts from your real material rather than inventing a company. Point it at a source and it writes from that.

## Nothing publishes without you

The agent drafts and schedules; it does not publish behind your back. Anything that would go public comes back as a task you approve or reject.

That is a hard rule, not a setting. An agent with your accounts and no approval step is one bad instruction away from a public mistake.

## Native per network

Ask for five posts across four networks and you get twenty drafts shaped for each, not the same text pasted four times. Length, tone, and hashtag conventions differ by network, and the drafts reflect that.

## Brands

Give the agent a [brand](./brand-voices) and the voice, palette, fonts, and assets all fold in. Without one, it writes in a neutral voice.

## From your phone

The agent is reachable from the dashboard and from Telegram, linked in **Agent → Channels**. Approving a draft from a phone while away from a desk is the point.

## Generating images and video

The agent generates visuals as well as text. You pick a quality tier, Draft, Standard, or Best, and never a model. Tiers that are not currently served are hidden rather than offered and then failing.

Generated assets land in the [media library](./media-library) like an upload, so they are searchable and reusable.

## Credits

Everything the agent does spends AI credits. The balance is in **Settings → AI Usage** and in the account menu. Running out returns a clear message and an upgrade link, and nothing is charged twice.

You can cap how much a single agent run may spend, so one ambitious request cannot drain a month.

## When it cannot help

Asked for something FoPost does not do, the agent says so and records it, rather than improvising something that looks like it worked. Those gaps are read directly as a build list.
