# Team Collaboration

## Inviting someone

1. **Settings → Team**
2. **Invite Member**
3. Their email, their role, and which workspaces they get
4. Send

They get an email invitation. Access begins when they accept, not when you send it.

## Roles

| Role | Can |
|:-----|:----|
| **Owner** | Everything, including billing and deleting the workspace |
| **Admin** | Manage the team, connect accounts, publish, approve |
| **Editor** | Create, edit, and schedule. Posts go for approval when the workspace requires it |
| **Viewer** | Read posts, the calendar, and analytics. Change nothing |

Roles are per workspace, so the same person can be an admin on one client and a viewer on another. That is what makes a single account workable for an agency with contractors.

## Approvals

A workspace can require approval before scheduled posts from editors go out. Turn it on in the workspace settings.

With it on: an editor writes and schedules, the post is held, an admin approves or sends it back. Nothing an editor writes reaches an audience unreviewed.

Leave it off for a team where everyone publishes. Turn it on the first time someone new joins.

The agent's drafts go through the same queue, so an AI-written post is reviewed exactly like a human-written one.

## Seats

| Plan | Seats |
|:-----|:------|
| Starter | 1 |
| Pro | 3 |
| Business | 10 |

Extra seats are $8/mo each on Pro and Business. Starter's single seat is a hard cap: to add anyone, move to Pro.

## Workspaces are the stronger boundary

A role limits what someone can do. A [workspace](./workspaces) limits what they can see. For an agency, put each client in its own workspace and grant membership per client, rather than relying on people to be careful in a shared one.

## Removing someone

Remove them from **Settings → Team**. Access ends immediately. Their posts and history stay: the work belongs to the workspace, not the person.

Revoke any [API keys](/api/authentication) they created separately. Removing a member does not revoke a key.
